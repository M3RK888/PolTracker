How this estimate works

Each race starts from a baseline: the state's partisan lean (Cook PVI) applied to the national environment. The environment is the average of the major poll aggregators' generic congressional ballot averages, currently .

The race's own polls are then blended in. Poll weight halves every days, rises with sample size, and is reduced when one pollster has several polls of the race. The baseline counts as fresh polls, so in a well-polled race the polls decide the estimate almost entirely.

Independents. Where the main challenger is an independent (Nebraska, Idaho, Montana, South Dakota), the estimate is that independent against the Republican, and a win counts on the Democrats' side of the seat count. They haven't all said who they'd caucus with.

Control. 65 seats aren't up this year (34 Democratic-caucus, 31 Republican). Democrats need 51 seats for a majority; Republicans need 50, since Vice President Vance breaks ties. Ratings use the same cutoffs as the 2028 map. This is a snapshot of the polls, not a forecast.
