What it can't show

• Only the polls part is tested. Past prediction-market prices and approval numbers weren't available, so the backtest uses polls alone. In the live forecast, 12 days out, polls carry 83% of the weight.
• Only the Senate. House districts are rarely polled, so they'd need a different test.
• Four elections is a small sample. All races in a year share one national polling miss, so this is really four big draws, not 134 independent ones.
• A few stand-ins. Past articles don't list Cook's partisan lean, so each state's lean comes from the two previous presidential elections; and the national mood comes from each year's own Senate polls.

The settings have not been changed to fit these results: 2026 is being forecast with the settings as they were, so its score is a fair test. They'll be revisited after it's scored. The code is backtest.py.

What the numbers mean

Called right: share of races where the side given more than 50% won. Easy to look good on, since most races aren't close, so it's also shown for competitive races (5% to 95%).

Brier score: the average of (forecast chance − outcome)², counting a win as 1 and a loss as 0. 0 is perfect; always saying 50% scores 0.250. Lower is better.

Inside the 80% range: each race's forecast includes a range expected to catch the result 80% of the time. Much less than 80% means the forecast was overconfident.
