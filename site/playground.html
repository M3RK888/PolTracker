How it works

This is an unvalidated hobby model. It uses a standard method, but its settings are judgment calls, and it hasn't been tested against past elections the way professional forecasts are. Treat the percentages as a rough guide.

The same simulation as the forecast. Your browser reruns the forecast's model: every race starts from the forecast's estimate, then gets a random miss. Part of the miss is national (shared by every race), part is regional (shared by states in the same part of the country), and part belongs to the race alone. Do that 20,000 times and you have 20,000 possible elections. With nothing picked, the odds here match the forecast pages (give or take a point of random noise).

Your picks keep only the elections where they come true. Say you pick Democrats to win Texas. The playground throws away every simulated election where they lose it, and counts everything from the rest. A Democratic win in Texas usually happens in a simulation where the national miss went their way, so in the elections that are left, Democrats also tend to do better everywhere else, especially nearby. That's why one pick moves the other states, not just the one you clicked.

Long shots need more simulations. If few elections match your picks, the playground keeps going in batches of 20,000 until at least 2,000 match (or 400,000 have been tried), and tells you how many it used. If fewer than 200 match, the model thinks your scenario is very unlikely, and the odds are too rough to trust.

Same picks, same answer. The random draws are seeded, so running the same picks again (or opening a shared link) gives the same numbers. Run again uses fresh draws, which shows how much is noise. The picks are saved in the page's address, so you can copy it to share a scenario.
